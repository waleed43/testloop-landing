The requirement

Play’s closed-test rule is simple. Meeting it is not.

If your personal Play Console account was created after 13 November 2023, every new app must complete a closed test before you can apply for production. Organization accounts are not subject to this rule.

What Google asks for

At least 12 testers opted into the closed test, and they need to stay opted in for 14 consecutive days. The production-access form also asks how testers were recruited and how engaged they were.

Invites alone are not enough. People drop out, forget to opt in, or never open the app.

What TestLoop is built to do

We recruit a buffer of 16 testers, match them by interest, country and performance, and track daily activity through an SDK you add to a closed-test build.

If someone goes quiet, they are reminded, then replaced — so the 12+ streak can keep running. You also get private day-7 and day-14 feedback, and a draft you can use when you apply for production.

How it works

Two sides. One 14-day loop.

Developers list an app. Testers get assigned by match and performance score. Activity is proven with the SDK, not screenshots of a home screen.

For testers

Get assigned. Stay active. Get paid in points or cash.

You do not pick from a random list. Apps are assigned to you when you are a good match.

  1. 1

    Set your device, country and interests

    Opt into Free Exchange, paid testing, or both. Paid cash work unlocks at Silver level, after verification, or as an approved pro.

  2. 2

    Opt in on Play, then link the app once

    Scan the QR or type the 6-character code. The SDK stores a tester token on the device — no personal data is sent.

  3. 3

The SDK

One line in your closed-test build.

An Android library written in Kotlin, and a Flutter plugin in Dart. Turn it on only for the build you upload to the closed track.

  • TestLoop.init(context, apiKey) — session heartbeats while the app is in the foreground.
  • Offline queue — missed pings are stored on device and credited to the day they happened (up to 3 days back).
  • INTERNET permission only. The SDK sends your API key, a tester token, foreground seconds, timestamps, and optional feature-event names you choose.
  • No personal data. No device IDs, accounts, location, or contacts.

FAQ

Questions worth answering clearly.

What is Google Play’s closed testing requirement?

Personal Play Console accounts created after 13 November 2023 must run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in for 14 consecutive days, for each app, before applying for production access. Organization accounts are not subject to this rule.

Is this once per account, or once per app?

Once per app. If you ship several apps from an eligible personal account, each one needs its own 12-tester, 14-day closed test.

How does Free Exchange work if I have no points yet?

You test other developers’ Free Exchange apps first. Each completed 14-day test (both feedback forms) earns 1–3 points. Listing your own app costs 12 points. Points are never cash and cannot be withdrawn.

Who decides how many points or how much cash a tester gets?

Final quality is 60% the developer’s private rating and 40% an automatic score (feedback length, evidence, and whether both forms arrived on time; for pros, test cases run, bugs filed, and recordings attached). If the developer does not rate within 3 days, the automatic score is used alone. Every completed test gets at least the minimum (1 point or $0.25; pros $5). Dropouts and missing feedback earn nothing.

What does the SDK collect?

Your API key, the tester’s TestLoop token, foreground session seconds, timestamps, and optional feature-event names you choose to track. It needs only the INTERNET permission. It does not collect device IDs, accounts, location or other personal data.

Can you prove a tester opted in on Play?

Google does not expose per-tester opt-in through an API. In TestLoop, “opted in” means the tester was assigned, has not been dropped, and is sending SDK heartbeats. You still paste the tester Gmail list and opt-in link into Play Console yourself.

Do testers leave ratings or reviews on Google Play?

No. Testers are rewarded for staying opted in, using the app, and sending private feedback. Rewards never depend on installs, ratings, or public Play reviews.

Is TestLoop live? Are payments working?

This page is for early interest. The product is a working prototype: payments and payouts are not connected to a live processor yet, and the SDK has not been published to Maven Central or pub.dev. The waitlist form on this page does not store your information yet.

Is TestLoop affiliated with Google?

No. TestLoop is an independent project, and TestLoop is a working name. Google Play and the Play Console are trademarks of Google LLC.
